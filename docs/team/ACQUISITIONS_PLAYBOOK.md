# Acquisitions playbook

This is the acquisitions-lead's knowledge base. It is written from the seat of a principal who buys and operates mobile home (MH) and RV parks in Arizona.

How to read it:
- Figures marked **(source)** come from a cited document as of November 2026.
- Figures marked **(rule of thumb)** are common industry practice and must be checked against the deal and current comps before anyone acts on them.
- Add lessons as you learn them, and say where each one came from.

---

## 1. How an operator-buyer makes money

A park is land plus infrastructure. Residents usually own their homes and rent the lot, so **lot rent is the business**. Value is net operating income (NOI) divided by the cap rate. Most upside comes from:

1. **Below-market lot rent.** Mom-and-pop owners often haven't raised rent in years. Closing the gap to market over 2–4 years is the core value-add. Arizona's rent-increase notice rules and resident relations limit the pace (§6).
2. **Utility bill-back.** A park that pays water, sewer and trash for residents can meter and bill them back. That lifts NOI without raising the headline rent.
3. **Filling vacant pads.** Bring in homes (new or relocated), sometimes as park-owned rentals or through rent-to-own programs. This costs capital and takes time.
4. **Expense discipline and professional management.**
5. **Refinance or sale** at a lower cap rate once the park is stabilized.

What makes a park worth more or less:

| Factor | Better | Worse |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Owned land | Ground lease |
| Water and sewer | City | Private wells, septic or lagoon (expensive, regulated, can kill a deal) |
| Ownership of homes | Tenant-owned | High share of park-owned homes (POH) |
| Density | Higher | Lower (Fannie's cap is roughly 12 homes per acre for existing parks, **source**) |
| Size | At least 50 sites (agency financing needs it, **source**) | Fewer sites |
| Streets | Paved | Not paved |
| Flood | Outside the flood zone | Inside |
| Location | Strong submarket | Weak |
| Age-restricted (55+) communities | Stable | Turn over more slowly |

**RV parks and resorts behave differently.**
- **Revenue:** much of it is nightly, monthly or seasonal rather than year-round lot rent.
- **Seasonality in Arizona:** winter visitors fill the parks roughly October to April, especially in Mesa and Apache Junction, Casa Grande, Yuma and Quartzsite. Summer occupancy collapses.
- **Valuation:** underwrite on trailing-12-month (T-12) revenue by month, never on a peak month.
- **Financing:** agency lenders rarely lend on transient RV income. Banks and SBA are the usual lenders.
- **Mixed parks:** MH parks with long-term RV spaces are common. Split the income by type.

## 2. Where off-market deals come from

Most good deals are never listed. Owners sell because of an event:
- **Debt pressure.** A loan maturing into much higher rates, a balloon payment, a lender that won't renew, or covenant trouble. This is the reason this app exists.
- **Age or estate.** The founder has aged out, the heirs want cash, or a partnership is splitting.
- **Burnout.** Management headaches, resident disputes, regulatory notices, or a failing water or sewer system the owner doesn't want to fund.
- **Taxes and liens.** Tax delinquency or mechanics' liens.
- **Distress on the record.**
  - Notice of trustee's sale (Arizona forecloses mostly outside court, through deeds of trust)
  - lis pendens, judgments
  - bankruptcy
  - water-system violations
  - code cases

**How a principal approaches an owner:**
- **Lead with:** who you are (an operator, not a broker), that you buy for the long term, that you take care of residents, that you can close, and that you're local.
- **Never** tell an owner what you think their debt situation is. It's presumptuous, it may be wrong, and it burns the relationship. Use the debt signal to decide *who* to call and *when*, not *what to say*.
- **Channels:** mail first. Many park owners respond to a personal letter. Then phone. Drive by and visit the office.
- **Persistence:** many deals come from the fifth or tenth touch over a year or two. Follow-up discipline wins.

## 3. Screening: what makes Jack pick up the phone

A park passes the first screen when:
- **Size and type:** it matches what we run (site count, MH vs RV, all-age vs 55+).
- **Location:** it's in a market we operate in, or near our existing parks (management efficiency).
- **Utilities:** city water and sewer, or a private system with a clean record.
- **Land:** owned land, not leased.
- **Upside:** lot rent visibly below market, or a fixable utility setup.
- **Timing:** there's a reason the owner might sell now (debt, age, distress).

Deal killers, or price cutters that need specialist diligence:
- failing septic or lagoon systems
- private water with repeated health violations
- floodway location
- a ground lease with a short remaining term
- a POH share above about 35% (that's Fannie's ceiling with a business plan; the general cap is 25%, **source**)
- environmental contamination
- a pending change of use or a hostile municipality
- a large deferred-maintenance list on roads and utilities

## 4. Underwriting

**Build NOI from the rent roll, not the seller's summary.**
- **Revenue:**
  - lot rent × occupied lots (reconcile the rent roll to bank deposits)
  - plus utility reimbursements
  - plus POH rent (separately)
  - plus fees
  - minus vacancy and collection loss (use at least 5% economic vacancy for agency sizing, **source**)
- **Expenses:**
  - property tax (reassessed after sale), insurance
  - utilities net of reimbursements
  - repairs and maintenance
  - payroll or on-site manager
  - management fee (budget one even if you self-manage)
  - administrative costs, reserves
- **Expense ratio (rule of thumb):** tenant-owned parks with bill-back run lower; master-metered parks and POH-heavy parks run higher. Check every line against the T-12 and comparable parks. Never accept a seller's ratio without support.
- **Value:** NOI ÷ cap rate. Cross-check against price per site.

**Benchmarks** (Northmarq Q2 2026, **source**):

| Measure | National | Arizona |
|---|---|---|
| Average cap rate | About 6.5% year to date; 6.5–7.0% in Q2 | — |
| Median price per space | $62,000 year to date, up 19% vs 2025 | — |
| Average lot rent | $800 a month, up 6.4% year over year | $795 a month, up 9.7% year over year |
| Occupancy | 94.9% | 96.4% |

Arizona was second nationally in year-to-date sales dollar volume. Use these as context only. Price a specific deal from local comps.

**The questions every underwriting screen must answer:**
1. Day-one NOI and cap rate at the asking or estimated price.
2. Market rent and the gap; stabilized NOI in year 3 after rent steps.
3. Debt: loan amount at the lender's DSCR and LTV limits; payment; DSCR on our loan.
4. Equity needed and cash-on-cash; a 5-year IRR on a reasonable exit cap.
5. The walk-away price: the most we'd pay and still hit our return.
6. For the seller: what they net after paying off their loan. This is often the real negotiation.

**Provenance matters.** A number from the rent roll, a number from a public filing, and our estimate must look different on screen. An offer built on an estimated maturity and an assumed rent is a guess.

## 5. Financing (what today's buyer and today's seller each face)

**Fannie Mae MHC** (term sheet, **source**):

| Term | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Max LTV | 80% |
| Min DSCR | 1.25x |
| Term | 5–30 years |
| Amortization | Up to 30 years |
| Rate | Fixed or floating |
| Recourse | Non-recourse with standard carve-outs |
| Park size | At least 50 sites; stabilized; Quality 3–5; age-restricted or all-age |
| Park-owned homes | Generally ≤25%, up to 35% with a reduction plan |
| Density | About 12 homes per acre for existing parks |
| Sponsor | A key principal with MHC experience |
| Prepayment | Yield maintenance or a declining premium |
| Assumability | **Typically assumable** |

**Freddie Mac MHC:** a similar agency program. Check the current term sheet before using its numbers.

**CMBS:**
- Loan terms: usually 10-year terms with balloons, often interest-only periods, and defeasance or yield maintenance for prepayment.
- Servicing: loans move to a special servicer on default or imminent default.
- Public data: loan-level data is public in SEC filings (the data-scout knows where).

**Banks and credit unions:**
- Loan terms: 5–10 year terms, often 20–25 year amortization, often personal recourse, and rate resets or balloons.
- Most mom-and-pop parks carry this kind of debt.
- Why it matters: **these maturities are the main source of seller pressure, and they are the hardest to see in public data.**

**Other lenders:**
- **SBA 7(a) and 504:** common for RV parks treated as operating businesses. Long, fully amortizing, so little maturity pressure.
- **Life companies:** low leverage on top-quality parks.
- **Debt funds and bridge lenders:** floating rate, short term, expensive. A bridge loan near maturity is a strong pressure signal.
- **Seller financing:** very common with older owners. It can be the deal-maker, because the seller gets payments instead of a lump sum.

**Pressure math:** refinance gap = current balance − the most a new lender will lend. The new loan is the lesser of:
- LTV × value
- NOI ÷ (DSCR × annual payment per dollar at today's rate)

When the gap is positive, the owner must bring cash, sell, or default. Show the gap, the rate it assumes, and where the NOI came from.

**Assumable low-rate debt** (agency loans are usually assumable) is valuable to a buyer and justifies a higher price. Always flag it.

## 6. Arizona rules that change deals

**The governing law.** Mobile home parks fall under the Arizona Mobile Home Parks Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (A.R.S. Title 33, Chapter 11, starting at 33-1401). The Arizona Department of Housing (ADOH) administers relocation and complaint processes.

**Change of use (closing or redeveloping a park), A.R.S. 33-1476.01** (**source**):
- **Notice:** written notice to the ADOH director and every tenant at least **180 days** before the change.
- **Rent:** no rent increase within **90 days** before the notice.
- **Moving payments to tenants:** the lesser of actual cost or **$22,500** for a single-section home and **$30,000** for a multi-section home.
- **Abandonment payments:** 40% of those amounts.
- **Ground-set homes:** up to $2,500 more.
- **Landlord payments into the relocation fund:** **$500** per single-section and **$800** per multi-section home for each tenant who files.
- **Penalties:** the same amounts again if the notice is late, or if a change of use follows other relocations within 270 days.
- **Disclosure:** after the notice, the closure must be disclosed to prospective buyers and tenants.
- **In practice:** redevelopment plays are slow, expensive and politically visible. An operator buys to keep the park.

**Rent increases:** ADOH's resident materials refer to a **90-day written notice** for rent increases (A.R.S. 33-1432, **source**: ADOH rent-increase packet). Confirm the current text before advising.

**Other rules:**
- **55+ communities:** age-restricted communities follow federal and state rules on age verification. ADOH has a separate packet.
- **Long-term RV spaces:** these have their own Arizona act, the Recreational Vehicle Long-Term Rental Space Act (A.R.S. Title 33, Chapter 19). **Verify the details before relying on them.**
- **Water:** much of central Arizona sits in Active Management Areas with assured-water-supply rules. Private water systems answer to ADEQ and EPA drinking-water rules. Check the system's compliance history for every park.

## 7. Due diligence checklist (after the LOI)

- **Financial:**
  - rent roll reconciled to leases and bank statements
  - T-12 and 2–3 years of operating statements
  - utility bills
  - property tax bills and the reassessment risk
  - delinquency and eviction history
  - rent-increase history
- **Physical:**
  - lot-by-lot inspection
  - age and condition of homes
  - roads
  - water and sewer system condition: camera the sewer, check well capacity and tests
  - electrical and gas distribution (who owns it)
  - drainage, flood zone
- **Legal:**
  - title and survey
  - zoning and legal nonconforming status
  - permits and licenses
  - leases and park rules
  - pending disputes or ADOH complaints
  - environmental Phase I
- **Homes:** titles for POH (homes are personal property), an inventory of abandoned homes.
- **Debt:** existing loan documents, assumability, the prepayment cost, estoppel.

## 8. Competitors for the same parks

- **Public REITs:** Equity LifeStyle Properties (ELS) and Sun Communities (Sun), both large in Arizona; UMH, mostly in the East.
- **Large private operators:** Cal-Am Properties (big in Mesa RV resorts), Havenpark, RHP, Inspire Communities, Kingsley, and many syndicators.
- **How they compete:** with low-cost capital and fast closes.
- **How a regional operator wins:** relationships, speed, local knowledge, flexibility on terms (seller carry, a longer close, keeping the family's name on the park), and reaching the owner first. **This app is that edge.**

## 9. What this app must do for me (test against this)

Every morning, in under ten minutes:
1. Who should I call today, and why now (one sentence, with a sourced fact)?
2. What changed overnight on parks I'm watching?

For any park, in under a minute:
3. Who owns it, and what else do they own?
4. What's the loan, when does it come due, how sure are we, and what would refinancing cost them today?
5. Is it in our buy box, and how far is it from our nearest park?
6. A first-pass value and a walk-away price, with every input's source visible.

Then:
7. Log the call, set the next follow-up, and move the deal stage in two clicks.

Anything that doesn't serve one of these is a candidate to cut.

## 11. What the 13 Nov 2026 screen walk taught

Source: first feature audit, demo clock pinned to 2026-11-12.

- The sentence above the dial is the one I act on. It has to say the same thing as the maturity note. A date or a range that has already passed, or a note that says the loan was most likely repaid, cannot be written as "ask what they have lined up to replace it."
- A blank balance is not zero. The same recorded name on two owner records is two records until someone links them. Show the other parks next to the name, and say they are the same name, not a confirmed owner.
- A stored balance of $0 is zero on file and prints as $0. A blank prints as —. Parks that only share the recorded name are listed beside the record. Their balances, sites, and park count are not added to this record's Debt on file, Sites, or Parks. Source: item 19 criteria, 13 Nov 2026.
- Confirmed on the demo walk, 13 Nov 2026. Silver Spur Holdings LLC stays two records. The RV dossier's Debt on file is —. The Havasu dossier's Debt on file is $3.2M and Sites is 120. The two records disagree on state, CO and CA, and the line says "Not a confirmed owner." Source: item 19 test.
- Do not put "you have held about N years" in a letter. That line is a score phrase.
- Confirmed on the call-line fix, demo clock 2026-11-12. Silver Spur Village says the Jul 2022 date has passed and the loan was most likely repaid. Canyon Village says the Sep 2023 to Sep 2026 range has already passed and the date is a model. Silver Spur RV Resort, still inside the window, still asks what they have lined up. The 74 percent on Canyon stays in the score note. Source: call-line test, 13 Nov 2026.
- A park is not a deal until someone saves a stage. The Stage control shows **Not in the pipeline** until then. That label is not a stage, and it is not a way to delete a deal. A next-action date typed before a stage is chosen waits; it does not create a Target. Choosing Target is a real save and puts the park on the board. Source: item 20 criteria, 13 Nov 2026.
